Views into Division: The Rise of Right-wing YouTubers in South Korea

Published August 3

A middle-aged YouTuber sits in front of the U.S and South Korean Flags, alleging that an opposition lawmaker’s aide is a North Korean Spy, while thousands of people watch behind screens. Pings go off as more and more people donate money to this YouTuber. Another channel that broadcasts to an astounding 2 million subscribers iterated that the Democratic Party Leader harbors “pro-DPRK sentiments.” These right-wing YouTube channels are decadent and often create content with one sole mission: to increase distrust in the opposition to consolidate power within right-wing parties.

The lingering question of what fueled the rise of right-wing YouTubers remains. The answer is simple: the rise of social media. 53% of South Koreans say they receive news via YouTube, which is higher than the average of 30% of countries, according to a 2024 report by the Korea Press Foundation (KPF). In addition, in a 2023 survey by the Chosun Ilbo newspaper, 70% of right-wing rally participants cited YouTube as their primary source. The videos are often representative of the rampant disinformation and conspiracy theories that have proliferated in recent years as YouTube has become the number one choice for conservatives who are often disillusioned with mainstream media.

Right-Wing YouTube channels in South Korea often label liberals as “Pro-North Korea,” leading to false resentment of liberals by the general public. Jinseongho Broadcasting, the most-followed ROK YouTube Channel with over 1.8 million followers, often links liberal politicians to North Korea, such as when the opposition leader Lee Jae-Myung used the expression “our North Korea” during a speech in January. The channel derided the politician, emphasizing that his slip of the tongue indicated “pro-North Korea” sentiments. The blatant misinformation found within right-wing YouTube channels has led to a surge in misinformed individuals with extremist opinions.

Dubbed as “Denture-tube”, a reference to older voters who often follow extremist rhetoric, these channels often echo the president’s views and convey false and unverified information. However, this information is often lauded by viewers for one sole reason: YouTube confirms bias. A recent study by Hankook Research indicated that people are more likely to believe in information if the YouTube channels create content that aligns with their own political beliefs. However, only 6.5% of people say they fact-check the information they receive from such a YouTube channel. Confirmation bias has aggrandized the influence of right-wing YouTubers in South Korea, as people with pre-existing political beliefs often tend to seek and find more videos that align with their political beliefs such as conservatives or liberals, leading to a lack of awareness of other political beliefs and values.

The Martial Law and the impeachment of Yoon Suk-Yeol have further sparked the rise of right-wing YouTubers. Right-wing YouTubers earn up to 21 million KRW (approximately 19,000 USD) for a single video just through donations made during the livestream. This cash cow has led to the rise of the right-wing YouTubers. In addition, political leaders such as Yoon Suk Yeol had repeatedly undermined the accuracy of traditional media sources, citing it as “inaccurate,” and instead urged supporters to gain accurate information from YouTube.

As the world navigates the complexity of polarization with the rampant spread of misinformation and disinformation-often fueled by social media, one thing remains clear: the public must remain vigilant and discerning in their pursuit of the truth. It becomes not only the responsibility of leaders but every citizen to help uphold the democratic values the nation is founded on and engage in informed dialogue. Only through mutual understanding can democracy in Korea endure.
